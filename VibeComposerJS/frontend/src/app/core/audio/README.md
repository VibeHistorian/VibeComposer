# Internal instruments and soundfonts

InstrumentSettingsService owns session-loaded SF2 banks and the selected instrument ID. Load files from Settings > MIDI; the transport selector offers TinySynth, Chiptune (TinySynth), plus the loaded filenames. Files are read and resolved in the browser, are limited to 250 MiB each, and are not persisted in project JSON or browser storage.

Chiptune is a bundled, sample-free TinySynth bank (`chiptune-patches.ts`), shared by playback, audition and offline export. It covers all 128 GM programs and notes 35–81 (82 uses maracas), with at most four operators per patch. `chiptune-reference.ts` describes the reference SF2's nineteen stationary A1 waveforms with 400 Fourier partials and maps 91 programs to their reference envelopes. `node scripts/derive-chiptune-waves.cjs` regenerates that file from the repository-root `chiptune_soundfont_4.0.sf2`; builds never read the bank. These derived patches retain delay, attack, hold, decay, sustain and release directly. They also preserve resolved fine tuning (including sample pitch correction) using TinySynth's frequency-ratio parameter t = 2 ** (cents / 1200), retaining a constant cents offset across the keyboard; Harp (46) is +7 cents. Coarse tuning is included in the ratio when present. The TinySynth adapter replaces their per-operator gain automation with linear stages matching this application's SF2 sampler, releasing from the current level even when note-off interrupts a stage. For example, Harp (46) retains its one-second decay rather than compressing it to an 87 ms exponential time constant. This targets the in-app SF2 renderer; other SF2 players can use different envelope curves or key scaling. Stock TinySynth and hand-authored chip patches keep their native exponential envelopes. Fourier waves retain their amplitudes with Web Audio normalization disabled. The remaining 37 sampled/multisample programs and the Standard kit use hand-authored chip approximations: sample attacks, evolving spectra, alternate banks/kits, filters and modulation are not reproduced exactly. The waveform data is a compact derived description, not bundled PCM or an SF2 loader.
Chiptune has its own editable `chiptune-loudness.ts` offsets, measured with the same probes as TinySynth. Run `pnpm run calibrate:chiptune` after changing patches to regenerate them (overwrites edits). Both bundled synths are ready immediately with normalization enabled; neither performs runtime calibration or uses SF2 cache entries. Selecting Chiptune requires no file upload and does not count toward the three loaded-bank slots. Engine selection stays a session preference and does not change saved GM programs or external MIDI.
Xylophone (13) uses four operators: a sine fundamental, a custom harmonic carrier emphasizing partials 4/6/9/11, a short FM attack on that carrier, and 3.36 Hz amplitude modulation of the overtones. Its 1.2-second exponential decay constant approximates the C4 sample's measured 7.2 dB/second decay, and both carriers retain the preset's -10 cents tuning. A 0.18-second release constant lets note length control the tail; voices stop 0.63 seconds after note-off. It does not use one-shot playback or add a reverb effect. This approximates the sampled signature rather than reproducing all eight recorded key zones or their baked ambience.
For operators with gain routing (`g > 10`), `relativeAmplitude: true` interprets `v` as modulation depth relative to the target carrier's velocity/key-scaled peak. The adapter resolves this separately for each note, preserving shimmer depth on quiet notes without changing the bundled patch or existing absolute-gain modulation. Matching modulator/carrier decay and release constants keeps that depth stable through the tail. `scripts/chiptune-xylophone-browser.test.cjs` measures rendered harmonics, decay, modulation depth across velocities, and note-off response; its diagnostics are written to `.angular/xylophone-patch-measurements.json`.

A successful load selects the new bank. At most three banks are retained in load order; loading a fourth removes the oldest. Failed loads preserve the selection and existing banks. Selecting a different instrument stops playback and disposes the old engine and decoded cache. Unloading the selected bank switches to TinySynth. Export captures a bank reference at its start so selection changes do not invalidate an in-progress export.

Sf2Bank resolves bank/program presets through instrument/sample zones, including key/velocity ranges, tuning, attenuation, pan, volume envelopes, loops and exclusive groups. Sf2InstrumentEngine shares decoded sample buffers within its context. Melodic programs use bank 0; drums currently use bank 128/program 0. A missing preset raises an explicit error.

Settings > MIDI offers instrument loudness normalization, off by default and saved as an app preference. Enabling it immediately prepares the selected engine and shows progress followed by a persistent readiness message inside Settings, without requiring Play. While enabled, loading or selecting a soundfont starts preparation for that bank; disabling or changing selection invalidates obsolete status updates. Calibration errors remain visible with retry instructions. Switching the preference stops internal playback and discards the previous engine. External MIDI and stored track volumes/velocities are unaffected. Export captures the preference and bank at its start.
TinySynth uses the bundled `tiny-synth-loudness.ts` table immediately, with no runtime measurements and no browser-cache lookup. Edit `TINY_SYNTH_MELODIC_OFFSETS_DB` (zero-based GM programs 0–127) or `TINY_SYNTH_DRUM_OFFSETS_DB` (MIDI notes 35–82, including shaker fallback). Each named row is an offset in dB: positive boosts and negative attenuates; 0 is unity. Rebuild/reload to hear edits across playback, audition and export; no cache clearing is needed. Manual offsets are applied as written, so retain headroom when boosting.
To recompute the baseline after changing TinySynth or its renderer, run `pnpm run calibrate:tinysynth` from `frontend/` with the protected browser broker running and `VIBE_BROWSER_BROKER=1` on this Windows host (or a supported local Chromium elsewhere). This explicit maintenance command uses the same raw measurement probes below with a fixed noise RNG seed, writes all 128 melodic and 48 drum offsets in dB with instrument names, and **overwrites manual edits**. Review the table diff before keeping regenerated values. The normal build never regenerates the table.
`instrument-calibration.ts` renders unity-gain stereo probes at 24 kHz through the same engine adapters used for playback. Melodic programs use MIDI 48/60/72 at velocity 100, 1.2-second holds spaced two seconds apart in a six-second window. Drums use four velocity-100 hits spaced half a second apart in a 2.5-second window, including the engines' shaker fallback. `@audio/loudness-lufs` measures the resulting PCM. Corrections target −24 LUFS, clamp to ±12 dB and additionally reduce gain if the measured sample peak would exceed −3 dBFS. Silence/fully gated input remains unity. Finite windows and a single velocity are approximations, especially for slow attacks, long releases or velocity layers; this is instrument balancing, not final mix loudness or clipping protection.
`NormalizedInstrumentEngine` applies fixed per-program melodic and per-note drum gains before the caller's mix bus, shared by playback, audition and export. Routes share gain nodes while voices overlap and release them when their final voice is disposed. The correction layer loads only when enabled; the meter loads only for soundfont calibration. Playback waits for pending preparation if started early and never clears the readiness message. Calibration failures prevent that audio operation and remain retryable; disabling normalization restores the original engine path.
Concurrent calibration of the same bank shares a promise. Completed soundfont profiles are cached in memory per loaded bank and in local storage by SHA-256 of the full SF2 contents. The versioned cache keeps at most eight profiles; changing renderer/probe/gain policy requires bumping its version. Cached arrays are validated before use. Storage denial still permits memory caching. Bank files and PCM are never persisted by calibration. Legacy TinySynth browser profiles are ignored in favor of the editable bundled table.

The pinned smplr patch adds per-region pan/scale tuning and delay/attack/hold/decay/sustain envelopes, with release from the current envelope level. Supported SF2 behavior is still a subset: modulators, LFOs, filter resonance/envelopes, key-scaled envelope times, linked stereo pairs and ROM/24-bit samples are not implemented. Mode-3 loops continue during release. Time parameters are capped at 40 seconds to fit export's tail allowance.

Tests use the local Super_Nintendo_Unofficial_update.sf2 under VibeComposerJS/ as a fixture without copying it into application assets. sf2-resolver.test.cjs covers all GM presets and the Standard kit plus synthetic inheritance fixtures. tiny-synth-browser.test.cjs renders all 129 presets independently and verifies the patch using controlled PCM. soundfont-loading.test.cjs checks file limits, invalid loads, selection and eviction; export-browser.test.cjs exercises the real settings file input, transport selection, eviction/unload and audio export.

Internal playback, overlapping note previews and offline export share a final soft peak limiter (output-limiter.ts), after master volume and pan. It preserves samples below 0.8 and smoothly saturates louder peaks toward +/-0.98, with no lookahead latency. Heavy overload adds harmonics; this is peak protection rather than transparent loudness compression. It applies to all internal instrument banks, independently of instrument normalization, and does not affect external MIDI or calibration probes. TinySynth voices also fade their final 5 ms before their scheduled stop to remove residual exponential-release discontinuities; patch envelopes and voice stop times remain intact. The synth browser suite measures dense chiptune mixes, quiet stereo transparency and voice endings.
